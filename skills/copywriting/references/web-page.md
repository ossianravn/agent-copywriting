# Landing pages and product pages

The reader arrived with a question and gives the page a few seconds to show that it has the answer. They scan the headline, the first line under it and the button, then decide whether to read on.

Their questions come in roughly this order. Answer them in this order:

1. What is this? The headline says what the product is or does, in the words the reader would use to search for it.
2. Is it for me? Name the reader's situation or task.
3. What will I get? Describe what they can do with it, with a concrete case.
4. Why should I believe it? Give proof: a number, a named customer, a screenshot, a guarantee. Use only proof the user has supplied, and mark each missing piece as `[NEED: …]`.
5. What does it cost, and what happens when I click? Say it near the button.
6. What do I do now? One main action per page, named for its outcome: "Start free trial", "Book a demo".

Write claims the reader can picture and check. "Sends the invoice the moment a job is marked done" tells them more than a general promise of efficiency. A claim that every competitor could also make gives the reader nothing to choose by, so replace it with what is true of this product only.

Keep each section to one question. Word the section headings as answers, so that someone who reads only the headings learns what the product does.

## Example

> **Invoices that send themselves when the job is done**
>
> For tradespeople who do their paperwork in the evening. Mark a job as finished on your phone, and the customer has the invoice a minute later.
>
> [Start free trial] 30 days, no card needed
