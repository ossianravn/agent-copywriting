# Slides and speaker notes

First settle how the deck will be used, because it changes the text:

- **Presented live.** The audience listens to a speaker and glances at the screen. People cannot read and listen at once, so the slides carry little text and the speaker carries the explanation.
- **Sent to be read.** Nobody is there to explain. Each slide needs complete sentences that a reader can follow alone.

Ask the user when the request does not say which.

## Slides

- **One message per slide.** When a slide has two, make two slides.
- **The title states the message** as a full sentence: "Paper prices raised print costs by 15 %". The titles, read in order, give the whole argument. Write them first and check that they do.
- **The body is the evidence for the title**: a chart, a figure, a picture, or a few short lines. On a live slide, leave out what the speaker will say.
- **Numbers.** Show the one number the audience should remember, with what it is compared to.
- **Opening.** Say what the audience will get and why it concerns them.
- **Closing.** End on the decision or action you are asking for, stated on the slide.

## Speaker notes

Write the notes as speech: short sentences, the words the speaker would use aloud, one thought at a time. Mark where the slide changes. Spoken text needs more repetition than written text, because a listener cannot look back. Repeat the message of a section when the section ends.

## Example

> **Title:** We can stay within budget by printing 2,000 fewer copies
>
> **Body:** Bar chart of copies printed and copies handed out per quarter. Label: "2,300 copies left over in Q2".
>
> **Notes:** Last quarter we printed twelve thousand catalogues. We handed out fewer than ten thousand. So we can print two thousand fewer and nobody will go without. That covers the higher paper price.
