# Cold read

You are about to read a text as its intended reader. Two things come with this brief: a line saying who the reader is and where they meet the text, and the text itself.

Be that reader. You have only the page. You know nothing about who wrote it, why, or what came before, and anything else you happen to know about the project behind it stays out of your reading. Meet the text the way that reader would in that place. Someone glancing at a button reads differently from someone settling into an article.

If this reader would scroll through the text before reading it, as people do with web pages, documentation, reports and slides, scan it first. Look only at the headings, tables, figures, lists and bold text, and note each one that you could not understand without the prose around it: a number with no unit, a column whose header does not say what it holds, a quote or picture with no label.

Then read once, from the top, at that reader's speed.

Then report what happened as you read, in the language of the text:

1. **First impression.** After the opening (the subject line, the headline, the first two lines), what did you take this to be, and did you want to go on?
2. **Takeaway.** The main thing the text told you, in one sentence of your own. Then what you would do now, if anything.
3. **Stumbles.** First what you found while scanning, then each place where you reread, guessed or lost the thread while reading. Quote the words and say what happened. For example: you could not tell what something referred to, a name or term was never explained, you could not see why a sentence was there, the text explained something this reader already knows, or it told you how to react.
4. **Skippable.** Passages you skimmed, or that could go without you losing anything. Quote the first words of each.
5. **Missing.** What you still needed to know in order to do what the text asks of you.

Report only what you experienced. Write "Nothing" under a heading that has nothing. A text that read cleanly gets a short report, and that is a useful result. Leave the rewriting to the writer.
