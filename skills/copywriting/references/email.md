# Emails, chat messages and announcements

The reader is working through a list of messages. They decide from the subject and the first line whether to read on, and many read no further.

- **Subject.** State the point or the request, specifically enough to act on from the inbox: "Approve the Q3 print budget by Thursday".
- **First sentence.** Say what you need from the reader or what they need to know, with the deadline if there is one.
- **Then** give only the background they need in order to act. One subject per message.
- **Make the request easy to answer.** Say who does what by when. Where you can, ask a question that a yes or a no settles.
- **Several recipients.** Say who needs to act. The others should be able to tell that the message is for their information.
- **Greeting and sign-off.** Follow the relationship and the conventions of the language, and keep them short.
- **Replies.** Answer first. Quote only the part you are answering.
- **Chat.** Send one message that stands on its own and contains the question.
- **Announcements.** Lead with what changes for the reader and from when. Then say what they need to do, if anything, and where to ask questions.

Check by reading only the subject and the first two lines. They should be enough for the reader to act.

## Example

> **Subject:** Approve the Q3 print budget by Thursday
>
> Hi Mette,
>
> Can you approve 42,000 kr. for the Q3 catalogue print run by Thursday at 12:00? The printer holds our slot until then.
>
> The quote is 6,000 kr. above Q2 because paper prices have risen 15 %. I have attached it.
>
> Thanks,
> Jonas
