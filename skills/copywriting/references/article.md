# Articles, posts, reports and documentation

The reader chose to open this, and can leave at any line. Many read the title and the first paragraph and then skim.

- **Title.** Say what the piece gives the reader, specifically enough that they can decide whether to read it.
- **Opening.** Give the point in the first paragraph. Someone who stops there should leave with the main thing.
- **Paragraphs.** One topic each, opened by a sentence that states it. The first sentences of the paragraphs, read in a row, should give the argument.
- **Order.** Follow the reader's questions. After the point they usually want to know why it holds, then what it means for them.
- **Headings.** Use them when the piece is long enough that readers will navigate it. Word each as a statement of what the section says ("Paper prices rose 15 % in 2026"), so the headings also work as a summary.
- **Reasoning in prose.** Keep "because" and "so" in sentences, where the links between ideas stay visible. Use lists for items that are parallel.
- **Evidence.** Support a claim where the reader may doubt it: a number with its source, or one example from the actual case.
- **Ending.** End when the last needed thing is said. Add a closing section only when it tells the reader what to do next.

For documentation, the reader is in the middle of a task. Start with what the page lets them do, give the steps in the order they perform them, and put explanation after the step it explains.

## Example opening

> **Paper prices raised our print costs by 15 % this year**
>
> Our catalogue cost 6,000 kr. more to print in the third quarter than in the second, and the whole increase comes from paper. This article shows where the price rose and what we are changing in the fourth quarter to stay within budget.
